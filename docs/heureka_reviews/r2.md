# r2 fresh-eyes review

### medicine_screen.js SCREEN! — FIX (roughly/no/roughly) — "SCREEN!" over a 100-animal grid with no visible sick animal; the end state (6 glowing animals, tiny storm clouds) does not read as "most alarms were false"
What a newcomer sees: a dense 10x10 wallpaper of animals, a glow blob in the middle, the word SCREEN! (an 8-year-old reads "screen" as a TV/phone screen, not a test). Rubbing the ring around is discoverable from the ghost hand, but nothing says why. At the end 6 animals glow, 5 get a 4px grey storm cloud, one duck smiles. Nobody can tell which one was sick or that the point is "6 alarms, only 1 sick". Level 2 is a wall of glow (30+ lit animals), also unreadable.
Fixes:
1. Make the payoff a visible count. When scanning finishes, the lit animals hop out of the crowd and line up at the clinic tent (big, bottom of screen). The doctor does a second test on each one: the single sick one gets big green spots + thermometer and a pill; each healthy one gets a big green ✓ and hops back to the crowd. Show a tally over the queue, "6 beeped → 1 sick" (L2: "31 beeped → 30 sick"). The before/after contrast between levels is the whole lesson, so it has to be readable at a glance.
2. Rename the command to "TEST EVERYONE!" or "SCAN THEM ALL!", and give the scanner a physical look (a beeping wand/stethoscope, not a thin white ring). Make the crowd smaller and bigger-drawn (e.g. 5x8 = 40, 1 sick) so individual animals and their reactions are readable; at 0.34 scale the expressions and clouds are invisible on a phone.

### space_trace.js TRACE! — FIX (roughly/roughly/roughly) — now has a dotted arc and a target ring, so the drag is findable; but nothing on screen says east/west or Earth spinning, and the target ring sits on the owl
Frame 0: dawn, glowing Sun on the left hill, a dotted arc over the barn, a dashed ring on the right. Much better than the owner's description: you can guess "drag the Sun along the dots". Problems: the dashed setting ring is drawn right around the sleeping owl, so it reads as "take the Sun to the owl" (wake it up?). The win text "Earth spins!" has no visual support at all: you dragged the Sun, so a kid concludes the Sun moves. One single drag per body, slow and easy (C is weak); the Moon round is a copy of the Sun round.
Fixes:
1. Put big "E" and "W" (or a sunrise/sunset compass) on the two hills, and move the setting ring off the owl onto bare hilltop. Optionally show a small spinning globe with the farm on it in a corner that turns as you drag (or as a 1 s win pull-back), so "Earth spins!" is earned; otherwise change the win text to "East to west!".
2. Make the trace demand some precision: the Sun only "counts" while it stays on the dotted path (dots light up as you pass them; leaving the path dims the day), so it is a tracing game, not a free drag.
Card: matches (east/west, highest at noon, Earth spins, winter path lower).

### build_shake.js SHAKE! — FIX (no/roughly/roughly) — the command names what the game does (quake), not what you do (stack blocks); the quake is barely visible in the frames, so "Wide base!" arrives with no visible test
In the films the tower reaches the flag, then nothing visibly shakes before "Wide base!". A newcomer never chooses "wide": the bot (and a kid) just stacks to the flag.
Fixes:
1. Rename the command (e.g. "QUAKE-PROOF IT!" or "BUILD TO THE FLAG!") and show the coming quake from the start: a rumbling table with motion lines / a little seismograph needle, so the kid knows a shake test is coming.
2. Make the shake big and obvious (table jolts ±10px, dust, cups rattle), and on a first attempt let the tall thin option be the tempting one (e.g. the flag is reachable with a thin stack of exactly the blocks in the tray), so the lesson comes from seeing a thin stack topple.

### build_standup.js STAND IT UP! — FIX (roughly/no/roughly) — beaver flips the flat planks onto their edges and walks across, but a flat plank is never seen bending, so "Beam depth!" is unearned
Frame 0: planks lying flat over the river with dashed hinge marks; the flip gesture is guessable. But the flat planks look perfectly fine, so there is no visible reason to stand them up, and the win frame (planks on edge, beaver crosses) looks the same as a bridge that was always fine.
Fixes:
1. Show the problem first: the beaver steps onto a flat plank at t=0 and it bows down into the water (big visible sag) before hopping back; then each plank stood on edge barely flexes when it walks over. A side-by-side sag is the whole lesson.
2. On win, have the beaver jump on the stood-up beam (bounce, no sag) to underline it.

### circuits_charge.js CHARGE! — FIX (roughly/roughly/yes) — hold-to-fill a jar to a gold line, release to snap a photo: good timing game, but there is no camera on screen, so the jar's link to the photo is invisible
The fill-fast-then-slow curve and the release precision are real fun. But a newcomer sees a battery, a glass jar and a grey box top-right; the photos just appear as polaroids. Nothing says "the jar powers the flash".
Fixes:
1. Draw a proper camera on a tripod in front of the stage with a flash bulb, wired visibly from the jar; on release the jar drains in a gulp, the bulb fires a big white burst across the stage, and the polaroid slides out (dark photo on an early release). 
2. Make "hold" readable at frame 0: a valve/tap handle on the pipe with a "hold" press-ring icon instead of the generic glow over the jar; and draw each next animal visibly farther back (smaller) so the higher gold line has a reason.

### circuits_latch.js LATCH! — FIX (roughly/no/roughly) — square wave, black chip, two rows of dots: a newcomer cannot tell which bit to store or what "storing" looks like
The tap target (glowing chip) is clear, but the frames do not show which row is the code and which is memory, the data's high/low is not mapped to lit/unlit in any obvious way, and "Flip-flop!" is jargon. I could not say what I did.
Fixes:
1. Make the data concrete: a conveyor of glowing / dark bulbs (or coloured beads) sliding under a camera-shutter chip; tap to snap the one under the shutter into the next memory slot. The code to match is shown as the lock's keyhole pattern right above the slots, same symbols, same order.
2. Stored slots must visibly freeze while the stream keeps moving (that contrast is the point: memory holds, data changes). If this can't be made readable, CUT and pick a simpler EE row.

### earth_overflow.js OVERFLOW! — FIX (roughly/roughly/no) — lamp melts ice on the rock and the tank spills; but the contrast that is the lesson (floating ice melts and the level does NOT rise) is never shown

### medicine_treat.js TREAT! — FIX (yes/no/roughly) — tap the bottle to dose a dish of green germs; most die, then the dish is empty with two white cells: the grey tough germs, which are the whole point, are tiny and never shown surviving a dose
