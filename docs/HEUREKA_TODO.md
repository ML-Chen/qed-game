## The owner's rules for every game (in order of arrival)
The base contract is docs/HEUREKA_MICROGAMES.md. On top of it, from playing the games:
11. (Added 07:28) No disguised multiple choice: never show 2–4 options (levers, buttons, cards, boards) where picking the "right" one wins. The player acts toward a goal; if a choice appears, it comes from acting (the light ball bounced off, so grab the heavy one), never as an answer to pick.

12. (Added 07:36, the owner: "one worry I have is if the action is too simple. Like in slide, instead of sliding one puck, which is effortless to do within the time, maybe you should slide three.") Every game needs real challenge within its time: the player does the action 2–4 times (three pucks, three leaks, three customers), each time a little different (a new position, angle, speed or obstacle), or one action that needs real precision or timing. Nothing may be winnable by one effortless move. Level 2 adds more repetitions or a harder variant. Tune `time` so a first-time player at level 0 just makes it, and keep the bot winning at L2 double speed.
13. (Added 07:36) The win banner names the idea in one or two words: `winText: 'Inertia!'`, 'Friction!', 'Dilution!', 'Resonance!', 'Fire triangle!'. The fail banner may name the idea the fail shows (`failText: 'Friction!'`) or stay a funny word. This is how players pick up the vocabulary without reading.
Apply 12 and 13 to the games you've already finished too (go back over them after your current game), then carry on.

14. (Added 07:47, the owner: "sometimes the action that's being asked of me is very unintuitive. TRACE sunrise/sunset, I had no idea what I was supposed to do. so I think for every level, should gesture to what you're supposed to do if the player is stuck. and ideally each level takes multiple taps or multiple swipes, and not just a single one.")
   - kit.js changed: SK.demo now shows at EVERY level whenever the player hasn't touched for 2–2.8 s (and at level 0 from the start until the first touch). So every game must call SK.demo(g, c, kind, from, to) LAST in draw, EVERY frame, at every level, pointing at the NEXT step in the current state (not a fixed spot), with the right kind (tap / drag / swipe / hold). Remove any `level` option or own level checks around it. If your game uses its own hint drawing, switch to SK.demo.
   - The first frame must make the action obvious without the hand too: the thing to touch glows (SK.glow), a drag has a visible destination (a dashed outline, a glowing slot, a path), a hold has a button or a thing that obviously gets pressed.
   - Multiple taps or swipes per level (rule 12): re-check every finished game.

15. (Added 07:50, the owner: "some of these are just straight up confusing like what are you doing, what does it mean, until you read the card. Thinking the FALSE ALARM one, for example.") The meaning must be visible WITHOUT the card: the result looks like the real-world outcome and the cause is on screen (the puck slid into the net because nothing rubbed on the ice; the pie grew but my slice thinned). If a concept can't be shown that way in a few seconds (statistics, abstract law or policy ideas), redesign it so the consequence plays out visibly, or replace it with a clearer row from BUILD_PLAN.md. Three fresh-eyes reviewers are reviewing every game now; the coordinator forwards their FIX/CUT notes — act on them before new games.

16. (Added 07:58, the owner on WIRE UP!: "this is very confusing tbh and unintuitive, feels like a multiple choice test where you have to guess the right answer.") If the player has to guess which connection, line, card, slot or target is the "right" one, the game is a quiz in disguise. The goal must be physical and obvious (fill the jar, get the puck in the net, stop the robot before the cake), and the concept must be what makes the obvious action work or fail. Abstract topics (app architecture, security, AI, law, policy) need extra care: act out a concrete thing ("you are the server carrying envelopes"), never "wire the right diagram". WIRE UP! is out of the build; heureka/games/PENDING (coordinator-owned) lists files left out.

17. (Added 08:00, the owner: "I'd like to give you permission to write more text, like label client, server, database. can write a command with more detail than just one word. no need to make it a puzzle." and "the command should not be enigmatic.") This REPLACES the one-word-command and three-words-on-screen limits:
   - The command is a short, clear instruction that says what to do and the goal, ALL CAPS, ending in "!", up to about 30 characters: "SLIDE 3 PUCKS IN!", "CARRY MAIL TO THE DATABASE!", "PUSH THE SWING IN TIME!", "LIGHT THE BULB!". The engine now wraps commands longer than 15 characters onto 2–3 balanced lines (src/engine.js `banner`). Test how yours wraps (look at the first frame). If a newcomer would ask "what does that mean?", rewrite it.
   - Label the important objects with their real names, so the scene explains itself: "Your phone", "Server", "Database", "Investor", "Battery", "Nucleus". Use SK.tag (bold, at least 18 px, dark pill) or lettering on the object itself. Labels name things; they don't give instructions or explanations (the command and the card do that). Keep it uncluttered: label only what the idea needs.
   - Never a puzzle with a hidden right answer (rule 16). With labels and a clear command, the player knows what to do and why; the challenge comes from doing it (timing, precision, several repetitions), not from guessing.
   Go back over all your games for 17 now: rewrite enigmatic commands and add the labels the idea needs.

18. (Added 08:05, the owner on the reactor game: "'Control / Critical' tells me nothing about what actually happened.") The win and fail banners must say what happened in plain words a newcomer understands, not jargon: "Steady power!" not "Critical!"; "Doubled!" not "PCR!"; "No gaps, no overlaps!" not "MECE!". A technical term is OK only if the game labelled it on screen first (e.g. "Chain reaction!" after neutrons visibly set off more splits). And the scene must show the real-world result the idea is about (the town's lights, the bridge holding, the jar filling), not just a meter.

19. (Added 08:08, the owner, with a screenshot of SPLIT THE NUCLEUS! where both the nucleus and the neutron glow with the same ring: "if you want me to drag something, that has to be obvious as opposed to just highlighting some things.") Every drag (and flick/fling) must show WHAT moves and WHERE it goes, differently: kit.js now has SK.grab(c, g, x, y, r) for the thing you move (solid pulsing ring + a yellow grab-hand badge), SK.target(c, g, x, y, r, {w, h}) for where it goes (a dashed, flowing outline with four arrows pointing in), and SK.arrow(c, g, ax, ay, bx, by) for the path between them (flowing dots + arrowhead). Use all three for every drag, in every game, drawn before SK.demo; don't use SK.glow on both ends of a drag any more (SK.glow is for "tap this"). They show at levels 0–1 until the drag starts, and at level 2 after 2.8 s idle.

20. (Added 08:12, the owner, with a screenshot of CHOP THE WORD INTO TOKENS!: "no game should have two consecutive variants in a row, like I shouldn't be doing 'unbelievable' three times in a row.") Every game needs several content variants (at least 4: different words, layouts, objects, positions, numbers of things), and two plays of the same game in a row must never show the same one. kit.js now has SK.variant(g, n): call it once in init to pick variant 0..n-1 (it uses g.rand and never repeats the previous play's variant; rewind-safe), and SK.shuffled(g, list). Within one game's repetitions (rule 12), each repetition must also differ from the one before. Apply to all your games.
